HermitSDR DSP and Warren Pratt’s WDSP 2.11

HermitSDR has its own native receive and transmit DSP engine. WDSP is a separate, widely used SDR library. They solve many of the same problems, but feature overlap does not establish identical sound or performance.

Our recommendation

Keep HermitSDR’s native engine and evaluate WDSP’s neural noise reduction as an optional receiver tool. We have measured encouraging isolated inference costs on Apple silicon. A complete audio adapter and matched listening comparisons are still needed before we can say how much it improves reception.

What changed in 2.11?

Warren Pratt’s library is officially named WDSP (sometimes called “WinDSP” in conversation). The October 15, 2026 release adds CW neural models, shutdown/lifecycle improvements, portability changes and fixes affecting several DSP components. Premium voice NNR arrived in 2.10; PureSignal 3.0, free-curve audio controls and broadcast FM stereo arrived in 2.00. These are different release milestones. See the official version history.

Have other SDR packages adopted it?

Yes, with important version differences:

  • piHPSDR’s development tree and deskHPSDR integrated 2.11 in October.
  • AetherSDR 26.10.3 bundles WDSP 2.10, with voice NNR; that does not establish adoption of 2.11’s CW models.
  • The Thetis PY2ECM fork’s October release includes 2.10-based neural voice reduction. This is a specific fork, not a claim about every Thetis distribution.

What have we measured?

On an M4 Pro, our October 20 offline probe measured four upstream NNR inference slots at approximately 2.02, 4.33, 2.22 and 2.60 milliseconds p99, within their respective 16, 16, 16 and 8 millisecond hop budgets. The probe used synthetic spectra and excluded FFTs, sample-rate conversion, audio queues and radio operation.

That supports further engineering work. It does not prove end-to-end latency, full receiver deadline safety or better intelligibility. HermitSDR’s current post-detector audio runs at 12 kHz; the neural model path needs a carefully measured 16 kHz bridge or another suitable insertion point. We have not completed a matched, blind recorded-audio comparison against Adaptive/MMSE or RNNoise.

Coming soon · Planned

Optional NNR for HermitSDR

The NNR build-out is on the roadmap. It is not included in the current DMG, and no delivery date is promised. Voice models come first; the new CW models require their own acceptance work.

Before shipping: resolve source/model distribution requirements, build the complete bounded audio adapter, measure CPU, memory and added latency under main/SUB load, and compare real recordings for intelligibility and artifacts. Model selection, missing-model handling and overload fallback must be clear to the operator.

Existing reducers remain available. The goal is a useful additional choice, with evidence about when it helps.

This comparison describes the reviewed versions and HermitSDR’s current implementation. Upstream quality claims are not an independent HermitSDR benchmark. No whole-engine migration or PureSignal 3.0 performance parity is claimed.
