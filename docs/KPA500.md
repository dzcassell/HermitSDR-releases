# Elecraft KPA500 — Station Devices driver

HermitSDR can watch and, within narrow limits, control an **Elecraft KPA500**
amplifier through the amplifier's own serial command set, under **Station →
Control Devices → Station Devices**. The driver is listed there as **Elecraft
KPA500 (RS232 PC port)**. It shows the amplifier's firmware and serial
number, operate/standby state, forward power and SWR, PA temperature, PA
supply voltage and current, band, and any fault the amplifier reports; it
offers **Operate** and **Standby** buttons and **Set band**; and it tells the
amplifier the band whenever the radio changes band. An amplifier fault stops
HermitSDR from transmitting until the amplifier clears it.

## Read this first: what has and has not been tested

- The driver was written from Elecraft's published documents — the **KPA500
  Programmer's Reference, Rev. A2** (ftp.elecraft.com, folder *KPA / Manuals
  Downloads*, file `KPA500 Programmers Ref.pdf`) for every command, and the
  **KPA500 Owner's Manual, Rev. D4 (2017)** for the fault table, the default
  data rate and the cable.
- It has been exercised only against a **simulated KPA500** inside HermitSDR's
  automated tests: the exact command strings, the reply formats, the band
  table, polling, operate/standby, band follow, faults and link loss, through
  the same serial code the app uses (on a pseudo-terminal).
- **Nobody has yet connected HermitSDR to a real KPA500.** The owner of the
  project does not have one. If you do, the things to check first are listed
  under *Trying it on a real amplifier*. Please send what you find to
  <https://github.com/dzcassell/HermitSDR-releases/issues>.

The RF2K-S and SPE Expert/Taurus drivers from the same request are **not**
included; for an SPE amplifier see the bridge in
[ReferenceAmplifierProtocol.md](ReferenceAmplifierProtocol.md).

## Cable and port

- Use the amplifier's rear-panel **RS232 (PC)** connector, not **RS232
  (XCVR)**. The owner's manual says the PC port takes "a standard 9-pin
  serial cable" to a computer, and that the XCVR port is for a Kenwood or
  Elecraft transceiver and cannot be used for firmware updates. A Mac has no
  RS-232 port: use a USB-to-RS-232 adapter (Elecraft's KUSB cable, supplied
  with the amplifier, is one), which appears as `/dev/cu.usbserial-…` or
  similar.
- Data rate: the owner's manual lists the `RS232 P` menu default as
  **38400** bps (choices 4800, 9600, 19200, 38400). The port is 8 data bits,
  no parity, one stop bit, no flow control. HermitSDR proposes 38400 when you
  pick the driver; if the amplifier's `RS232 P` menu has been changed, match
  it.
- One program at a time. HermitSDR asks macOS for sole use of the port; the
  Elecraft KPA Utility or any other program must be closed. The KPA500's
  **XCVR** port can still be connected to a transceiver; that side is
  untouched.
- The amplifier answers nothing while it is switched off (standby power only).
  HermitSDR then shows CONNECTING / RECONNECTING with "no response" in the log
  until the amplifier is on. HermitSDR never switches the amplifier on or off.

## Setting it up

1. Open **Station → Control Devices → Station Devices**, press **Add…**.
2. **Driver**: `Elecraft KPA500 (RS232 PC port) (Amplifier)`. The link
   switches to **Serial** at 38400.
3. **Port**: pick the adapter from the list (or type its `/dev/cu.…` path).
4. **Expected model**: leave blank, or type `KPA500` — the driver always
   reports that word (the amplifier's running firmware has no model query), so
   anything else faults the device.
5. **Poll interval**: 1 s is the default and fine. Each poll is one 29-byte
   write of six queries; 2 s is gentler if you also watch the amplifier's own
   display.
6. **Hot at** and **Inhibit TX while offline** behave as for any amplifier
   (see [StationDevices.md](StationDevices.md)). The KPA500 reports its PA
   temperature in whole degrees; 80 °C is the default limit.
7. **Add**. Within a second the Identity box shows `KPA500`, the firmware
   (for example `01.54`) and the serial number; the Telemetry box fills on
   the next poll.

## What is sent and when

| HermitSDR sends | When | Amplifier answers |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `^RVM;^SN;` | Once per connection | `^RVMnn.nn;` `^SNnnnnn;` |
| `^OS;^WS;^TM;^VI;^BN;^FL;` | Every poll interval | `^OSn;` `^WSppp sss;` `^TMnnn;` `^VIvvv iii;` `^BNnn;` `^FLnn;` |
| `^OS1;` / `^OS0;` | Operate / Standby button | nothing (the next poll shows the state) |
| `^BNnn;` | Set band button, or the radio changed TX band | nothing |

Formats, from the Programmer's Reference: `^WS` is watts then SWR with an
implied decimal after the second SWR digit (`^WS512 015;` = 512 W, 1.5:1;
SWR `000` means "not transmitting" and is shown as a dash); `^VI` is volts
and amps each with an implied decimal after the second digit (`^VI524 123;`
= 52.4 V, 12.3 A); `^TM` is °C; `^BN` codes are `00` 160 m, `01` 80 m, `02`
60 m, `03` 40 m, `04` 30 m, `05` 20 m, `06` 17 m, `07` 15 m, `08` 12 m, `09`
10 m, `10` 6 m — the amplifier ignores any other value, and HermitSDR sends
nothing for a band outside this table.

Never sent: `^FLC;` (clear fault), `^ON0;` (power off), the boot-mode `P`
(power on), and the per-band ALC, power-adjustment, fan, T/R-delay, speaker,
inhibit-input and data-rate settings. Those stay with the front panel and
the KPA Utility.

## Band follow and manual override

HermitSDR hands the driver the dial on every change and once when the
amplifier connects. The driver sends `^BNnn;` **only when the radio's
transmit band changes** (and the amplifier does not already report that
band). Tuning inside a band sends nothing; a frequency between amateur bands
sends nothing. If you press **Set band** to put the amplifier somewhere else,
that choice holds until the radio next changes band — the radio's band
change always wins.

The transmit frequency is used (VFO B under split), transverter-mapped to
the RF side, so an amplifier on a transverter IF is told the IF band.

Note the amplifier's own `BC` ("STBY on band change") menu: when it is on,
every band change — including the ones HermitSDR sends — leaves the
amplifier in standby until you press Operate.

## Faults and the TX interlock

Each poll asks `^FL;`. `^FL00;` means no fault. Any other number raises an
alarm named `FLnn` with the owner's-manual text:

| `^FL` | LCD | Meaning (Owner's Manual Rev. D4, p. 18) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 02 | HI CURR | Excessive power amplifier current |
| 04 | HI TEMP | Power amplifier temperature over limit |
| 06 | PWRIN HI | Excessive driving power |
| 08 | 60V HIGH | 60 V supply over limit |
| 09 | REFL HI | Excessive reflected power (high SWR) |
| 11 | PA DISS | Power amplifiers dissipating excessive power |
| 12 | POUT HI | Excessive power output |
| 13 | 60V FAIL | 60 V supply failure (cycle mains power after correcting) |
| 14 | 270V ERR | 270 V supply failure (cycle mains power after correcting) |
| 15 | GAIN ERR | Excessive overall amplifier gain |

A number not in the table is shown as "fault nn" — look at the amplifier's
LCD. While a fault is raised the Station Devices strip reads **INHIBIT** and
HermitSDR refuses to transmit (PTT, TUNE, CAT, key, control surfaces); it
does not change ARM and never clears a radio-side refusal. The alarm clears
when the amplifier next reports `^FL00;`, which happens after you clear the
fault at the amplifier (tap OP/STBY per the owner's manual). HermitSDR does
not send `^FLC;`.

Attenuator-level faults that the amplifier clears by itself may appear and
disappear between polls; the log keeps each one. The "communication failure
between KPA500 and K3" entry (fault 0) is the same number the amplifier uses
for "no fault", so it cannot be told apart over this port.

Treat the inhibit as a courtesy, not as protection: it reacts within about one
poll interval, far slower than the amplifier's own circuits.

## Trying it on a real amplifier

Please check, in this order, and report:

1. The Identity box shows `KPA500`, a firmware like `01.54` and the serial
   printed on the amplifier.
2. Telemetry agrees with the front panel: band, temperature, supply volts;
   power and SWR while you transmit through it from another exciter.
3. **Operate** and **Standby** change the front-panel state within a poll.
4. Changing band on the radio changes the amplifier's band (watch the
   amplifier go to standby if its `BC` menu is on).
5. A fault (for example disconnect the output and drive it gently, if you are
   comfortable doing so) shows the right `FLnn` and **INHIBIT**, and clears
   after the amplifier is reset.
6. Whether the amplifier accepts six queries in one write, as sent here, or
   needs one query per write — if telemetry rows stay at a dash while the
   log shows frames arriving, that is the first thing to suspect.

Nothing in this driver keys the radio, changes drive, or arms the
transmitter; the only path into transmit is the inhibit described above.
